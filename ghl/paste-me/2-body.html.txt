        <p class="ech-eyebrow">Improving the inputs</p>
        <h2>We spend all our attention on the output. What about the person who has to produce it?</h2>
        <p>Luke says that Jesus &ldquo;increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.&rdquo; We don&rsquo;t know everything He was doing through those years of preparation, but the pattern is plain enough: growth that was intellectual, physical, spiritual and relational &mdash; all of it before the ministry began.</p>
        <p>There is a parallel in that for founders. We spend nearly all of our attention on the output &mdash; revenue, growth, leadership, strategy, decisions, execution &mdash; and very little on developing the person who has to produce it. Are we still growing in wisdom? Are we taking care of our physical health and our energy? Are we staying spiritually grounded and connected to God? Are we strengthening the relationships and the people around us?</p>
        <p>Those things aren&rsquo;t separate from entrepreneurship. They are part of what equips us for it. Building a company is a roller coaster, and you hear a great deal about the wins, the exits and the breakthroughs &mdash; far less about the fear, the losses, the difficult decisions, the uncertainty, and the seasons where things simply don&rsquo;t work. Those seasons come.</p>
        <p>So part of preparing to be a founder, and part of lasting as one, is asking: what am I doing now to become the kind of person who can carry what this journey is going to require of me? When your spiritual life, your physical health, your mental and emotional wellbeing, your wisdom and your relationships are being strengthened on purpose, you are far better equipped to persevere in faith, stay clearheaded under pressure, make the hard decisions, and keep moving forward when it gets difficult.</p>
        <p><strong>That is where David and Derek open the masterclass &mdash; before a single tactic.</strong></p>

      <h2>Your Meta ads don&rsquo;t need a bigger budget. They need the other two arms working.</h2>
      <p class="ech-lede">Most Q4 plans treat paid social as the entire strategy: raise the budget in November and hope it clears. But your cost per acquisition isn&rsquo;t decided inside the ad account. It&rsquo;s decided by what happens after the click, and after the first order.</p>
      <div class="ech-fbar">
        <p class="ech-formula">Ads <span class="ech-x">&times;</span> Offer <span class="ech-x">&times;</span> Repeat <span class="ech-x">=</span> the CAC you can afford</p>
        <p class="ech-fbar-note">Each arm makes the other two cheaper</p>
      </div>
      <div class="ech-cols-3">
        <div class="ech-card">
          <h3>Paid social</h3>
          <p>What your Q4 creative has to communicate, which offers to put spend behind, and when to scale &mdash; so you&rsquo;re not paying peak CPMs for a message that was never going to convert.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="ech-card">
          <h3>Offers &amp; AOV</h3>
          <p>A stronger offer lifts conversion rate, so the same clicks buy more customers. A bigger basket means you can afford to pay more for a customer than the competitor bidding beside you.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="ech-card">
          <h3>Email &amp; SMS</h3>
          <p>The second and third order don&rsquo;t carry an ad cost. Flows and campaigns that bring buyers back are what pull your blended CAC down across the whole quarter.</p>
